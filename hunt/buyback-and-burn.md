# Buyback & Burn

**Built by products. Recorded in HUNT.** HUNT supply has only ever moved in one direction —
down. This page covers both halves of that: the **verified historical record** of supply
already removed, and the **forward-looking, discretionary** buyback approach funded by
product revenue.

The two are distinct, and it matters not to blur them.

## The historical record

Since 2020, **16 recorded supply changes** have permanently removed **301,087,312 HUNT** —
roughly 60% of the original 500,000,000 issuance. Every entry has a public record, most as
an Ethereum transaction.

| Category | HUNT removed | What it was |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End of ecosystem bounties (2020) | 186,578,670 | Retirement of the unissued bounty allocation |
| Team allocation & second-round funding (2020) | 100,000,000 | Retirement of the corresponding allocation |
| HUNT → MINT swaps (2021) | 10,775,286 | Community (7,183,524) and team matching (3,591,762) swaps at the Mint Club launch |
| Quarterly fee-revenue burns (2020–2022) | 3,733,268 | 11 quarterly burns funded by non-HUNT fee revenue |
| Historical supply reconciliation | 88 | Residual closing the historical entries to canonical supply |
| **Total** | **301,087,312** | 16 recorded changes |

The full ledger — every event, date, reported value at execution, and its transaction or
announcement link — is published at **[hunt.town/hunt](https://hunt.town/hunt)**.

> Reported USD amounts in that ledger are **contemporaneous values at the time of
> execution**, not a current market valuation.

The two large 2020 entries are structural: they retired allocations rather than buying
tokens back from the market. The quarterly 2020–2022 entries are the precedent for what
follows — supply removed using revenue the products actually earned.

## Revenue into HUNT, going forward

The forward-looking approach is simple in shape:

1. **Revenue accumulates.** Products generate revenue over time.
2. **The team initiates a buyback.** Timing and size are decided case by case.
3. **HUNT is burned.** Purchased tokens are permanently removed from supply.

Because HUNT has no minting function, a burn is irreversible — burned supply cannot be
reissued. See [Supply & Distribution](supply.md).

## Discretionary by design

This is the part to read carefully, because it is where token projects most often
overpromise.

- Buybacks are **occasional and discretionary**. They happen when the team decides that
  accumulated revenue justifies one.
- There is **no fixed schedule**, no committed cadence, and **no guaranteed allocation** of
  revenue to buybacks.
- Amounts depend on accumulated revenue and the team's judgement at the time.
- Future discretionary buybacks are **separate from the historical record above**, and each
  completed event will get its own public record.

Nothing here is a commitment to buy, a yield, or a return. It describes how the studio
routes product revenue when it chooses to act. See [Terms](../terms.md).
