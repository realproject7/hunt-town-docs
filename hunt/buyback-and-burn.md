# Buyback & Burn

**Built by products. Recorded in HUNT.** The studio's products generate revenue. That
revenue is what funds HUNT buybacks — the team buys HUNT on the open market and permanently
burns it.

This is the second way HUNT connects the economy. The first is structural: HUNT sits
underneath the tokens the studio launches (see [HUNT as the Reserve Token](reserve-token.md)).
This one is economic: **what the products earn flows back into the token.**

## How it works

1. **Revenue accumulates.** Products generate revenue over time.
2. **The team initiates a buyback.** Timing and size are decided case by case.
3. **HUNT is burned.** Purchased tokens are permanently removed from supply.

Because HUNT has no minting function, a burn is irreversible — burned supply cannot be
reissued. See [Supply & Distribution](supply.md).

## Discretionary by design

This is the part to read carefully, because it is where token projects most often overpromise.

- Buybacks are **occasional and discretionary**. They happen when the team decides that
  accumulated revenue justifies one.
- There is **no fixed schedule**, no committed cadence, and **no guaranteed allocation** of
  revenue to buybacks.
- Amounts depend on accumulated revenue and the team's judgement at the time.

Nothing here is a commitment to buy, a yield, or a return. It is a description of how the
studio has chosen to route product revenue when it does act. See [Terms](../terms.md).

## Public record

Each **completed** buyback-and-burn event gets its own public record, so the history can be
checked rather than taken on trust. The record of executed events is published on the studio
site at **[hunt.town/hunt](https://hunt.town/hunt)**.
